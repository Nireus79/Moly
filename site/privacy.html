Moly privacy policy

Last updated: 13 October 2026. This policy covers the Moly browser extension, the Moly app that runs on your computer, and the Moly app for Android.

In short. Your conversations stay on your computer. The people who make Moly receive nothing: no account, no analytics, no telemetry, no tracking, no advertising network. Text leaves your computer only if you choose a cloud AI provider, and then it goes to that provider with your own key.

Who we are

Moly is published by Hermes Software (GitHub: Nireus79) as open-source software under the MIT licence. Contact: Hermes_creative@proton.me.

What Moly stores, and where

WhatWhere
Your conversations and messages, the people you mention, goals and facts Moly learned from what you wrote, your settingsAn encrypted database (AES-256) in the .moly folder of your user folder on your computer. The key is kept in your operating system's keychain; on a computer without one, you choose a passphrase that protects it (or, knowingly, a key file).
The AI provider you chose and, if you use Claude or OpenAI, your API keyThe same encrypted database. You can protect the key with a passphrase that Moly never stores. Moly never shows the key again and never writes it to a log.
A session token for your local profile and your interface preferencesThe browser's extension storage. The API key is not kept there.
Diagnostic logsFiles in the .moly folder and in Moly's configuration folder. They do not contain your message text, names or e-mail addresses.

Moly has no account on any server of ours. On a computer, the first time you use it you make a profile with a name and a password (no e-mail address is asked), and Moly asks for the password whenever the browser has been closed. If someone else uses Moly on the same computer, Settings can make another profile for them. The profiles stay on your computer, with its own conversations, settings and AI key. Moly shows a recovery code once, for a forgotten password; only a hash of it is kept, and Moly cannot recover a password for you. The start screen lists the names of such profiles. The phone app has one profile only and no password of its own.

What leaves your computer

  • With Ollama (a model on your computer): nothing.
  • With Claude (Anthropic) or OpenAI: the messages you write, together with the context Moly needs to understand them (for example a short summary of the conversation and the people you mentioned), are sent to that provider to write the replies. This uses your own API key and is governed by that provider's terms and privacy policy. Moly asks you to confirm this before you save the key. Card numbers and bank account numbers are removed from your text before anything is sent. Other secrets you type (such as passwords) are removed from what Moly stores and from later requests, but the first analysis of a message reads it as you wrote it, so do not type passwords into Moly.
  • Moly sends nothing else anywhere. It has no analytics, no crash reporting to us, and no advertising network.

The small card

In its own area below the writing box, Moly may show a small card labelled "Sponsored": a message from us (it carries our name and logos) or from a sponsor. Its text comes from a small data file on our website (see below) or, if that is unavailable, from the app itself; it is chosen by the day and never by anything you wrote, and it never appears between messages. You can hide it with its Hide button; it comes back the next time you open Moly. If you click its link, that website opens in a new tab and sees your visit, like any link you click; we learn nothing about you from it.

Why the extension needs its permissions

  • Side panel: to show Moly beside the page you are working on.
  • Storage: to remember your session and interface preferences.
  • Native messaging: to start and stop the Moly app on your computer. The extension cannot do anything on your computer except talk to that app.
  • Access to http://127.0.0.1:11436: to talk to the Moly app on your own computer. The extension does not read the web pages you visit; it talks to the Moly app on your computer (and, to look for it, to Ollama on your computer). When you remove the extension, your browser opens a page of this website (uninstall.html) that explains how to remove the Moly app too; Moly sends nothing in doing so. It also asks GitHub (api.github.com) for the newest Moly release, when the install screen is shown and when you open the “Moly app” section of Settings, to offer the installer or an update. When you press the button, the extension downloads that installer from the project’s GitHub releases and asks the browser to open it; for this it asks the browser for the optional downloads permission at that moment, and uses an ordinary download link if you refuse. That request contains none of your data; GitHub sees your IP address, as any website you open does. The extension also fetches, at most once a day, a small data file with the text of the “Sponsored” card (nireus79.github.io/Moly/cards.json), so the card can change without a new version. It is plain data, never code, and the request carries no cookies, no referrer and nothing about you; the built-in card shows if the file is unavailable. Moly for Android makes the same card request (not the GitHub one).

The Android app

The phone app is the same Moly with the same Moly engine inside it, and everything above holds, with these differences.

  • Where your data is: in the app's private storage on your phone, in an encrypted database (AES-256). The database key is created on the phone and protected by the Android Keystore; it never leaves the phone. Android's backup is switched off for Moly, so your data is not copied to a cloud backup. Uninstalling Moly deletes it. Settings, "Save a copy" writes it to a file in a place you choose, and "Restore from a copy" reads such a file back; Moly sends neither anywhere.
  • What leaves the phone: only what you send to the provider whose key you saved (Claude or OpenAI), as described above. There is no Ollama on the phone. Moly has no analytics, no crash reporting and no advertising network in the version published on GitHub.
  • Permissions: Internet, to reach your AI provider (and for the app's own connection to its engine on the phone); foreground service and notifications, so a reply that is being written can finish while you are in another app (Android shows a small "Moly is running" notice while it runs; Moly stops it by itself a few minutes after you leave the app, and starts it again when you open Moly). Moly asks for nothing else: no contacts, no location, no microphone, no camera, no access to your files or to other apps. It never reads what is on the screen of another app. You give it text by pasting it or by sharing it to Moly.
  • The engine on your phone: it listens on your phone only, on a port that only your copy of Moly uses, and it answers only requests that carry a token that only the Moly app can read.
  • "Report a problem or a reply": Settings can prepare a short note (phone model, Android version, when Moly's engine started and stopped, error lines). It is built to leave your messages out. You can add a note of your own, for example a reply from Moly that you want to report; you can paste it there. You read everything first. It goes nowhere unless you tap Send: your mail app then opens with the text and our support address (Hermes_creative@proton.me), and you press Send there. If the phone has no mail app, the share sheet opens and you choose where it goes.

Your choices

  • Export: Settings, "Export my data" saves everything Moly stored about you as one file.
  • Delete: Settings, "Delete profile permanently" removes your conversations, contacts and everything learned from them. Uninstalling the app keeps your data unless you ask for it to be deleted too: the Windows uninstaller asks you; on Linux and Mac use uninstall.sh --purge.
  • Remove your AI key at any time in Settings.
  • Because the data is on your computer, you also control it with your own tools: back it up, copy it, or delete the .moly folder.

Security, and its limits

The local app listens on your computer only, answers only the Moly extension, and requires a token created each time it starts. The database is encrypted. These measures protect your data from other websites and from other people who find your files. They cannot protect against malicious software that runs as you and can read your keychain or watch your keyboard. Use a passphrase for your API key, create a separate key for Moly with a spending limit at your provider, or use Ollama if no key should exist at all. If you lose the keychain entry or forget the passphrase (for example by reinstalling your operating system) the database cannot be opened, and nobody can recover it for you, so export your data first.

Moly is not a crisis, medical or legal service

Moly is a writing coach. It gives no emergency, medical or legal advice and no emergency contact numbers. If you or someone else is in danger, contact your local emergency services.

Children

Moly is not designed for children.

Changes

If this policy changes, the new version will be published here and, if the way your data is handled changes, Moly will tell you inside the extension.
